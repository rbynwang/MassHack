MassHack · Energy track · proposal for the team

Feel the weather, imagine the bill, then act.

A small world model for a commercial building with solar and a battery. It learns how climate drives the building's power, imagines what tomorrow costs under each possible battery plan, and executes the plan whose imagined future is cheapest. Then it checks reality against its imagination.

Research question: how much of the best possible bill saving can a climate-aware world model capture, compared with the battery setting most installers ship, especially on the hot days that set the monthly peak?

Weather is the biggest single error in predicting load, and acting on price alone makes peaks worse. That is the gap a climate-aware world model fills.

The model

A world model with three parts: see, imagine, choose.

"JEPA-like" here means the model predicts the building's next state in a learned embedding, conditioned on the weather and on the action, instead of only forecasting one number. Small read-out heads turn that embedding into kW and dollars so the planner can compare futures.

learned from the dataset (PyTorch on the Mac)exact equations, not learnedthe decision loop
What it learns

Climate → load and solar. Hot afternoons raise cooling load; cloud cuts PV. This is where the data has rich variation, and where judges will see "it understands climate".

What it does not need to learn

Battery and bill math. SOC, efficiency, and the tariff are known equations. Keeping them exact makes every imagined dollar trustworthy and the model small enough to train in minutes.

The one limit to state plainly

Action effects on load need varied actions in the data. If the dataset never pre-cools, the model cannot learn what pre-cooling does. Batteries are safe; HVAC actions need a simulator (path 3 below).

The 2-minute demo

Four beats, one building, one hot day.

  1. The problem

    A Massachusetts office with solar and a battery. Its bill is set by the hottest 15 minutes of the month, and the battery app doesn't know the weather.

  2. The model sees climate

    Idea C: the learned map of days. Prediction accuracy vs. a naive forecast, on held-out hot days.

  3. Heatwave, live

    Ideas A and B: imagine plans, pick one, play the real day. The vendor battery runs empty at 4 pm; ours doesn't.

  4. The number

    Over the summer: % of the best possible saving captured, $ per month, peak kW avoided. One line on what comes next.

Three ways to build it on one Mac

How we prove the model predicts
  • Split by time: train on earlier months, test on the hottest held-out weeks.
  • Load and PV error (MAE, P10–P90 coverage) vs. a seasonal-naive forecast.
  • Ablation: remove climate inputs and show the error rise.
How we prove the decisions pay
  • Same days, five strategies: no battery, vendor default, time-of-use rule, world model, perfect-foresight optimum.
  • Headline: % of the optimum's saving captured, split into energy $ and demand $.
  • Monthly peak kW and the days the vendor default set a new peak.
